Web Design Brisbane How to Choose Explained

The first filter is the commercial task, not the technology label. Brisbane website work commonly spans custom design, small business websites, WordPress builds, Shopify or WooCommerce stores, redesigns, landing pages, SEO-friendly builds and care plans. Each exists to solve a different problem.

  • New business: a clear service presence and an enquiry path matter more than page count.
  • Established service business: landing pages, service pages and local pages should support the demand you already have.
  • Existing site owner: a redesign must modernise an underperforming site without losing search visibility already earned.
  • Online seller: the store platform decision turns on products, update frequency and fulfilment, not appearances.

If the brief cannot name the job in one sentence, it is too early to request pricing.

Six questions to ask before comparing quotes

Most disappointment comes from comparing quotes that were never scoped the same way. Before any designer prices the work, answer these in writing:

  1. How many pages or page templates are essential at launch?
  2. Will the site run on an editable platform, such as WordPress, so updates do not need a developer on call?
  3. Is store functionality needed now, or later, and on which platform?
  4. Is copy supplied, or is copy support part of the brief?
  5. What must be protected in a redesign, including useful content and ranking signals?
  6. What happens after launch: care plan, training, backups, maintenance?

A business with prepared copy, a settled service list and one enquiry goal is far easier to estimate than one still deciding offers, audiences and page structure. Reading a quote: lean or simply missing work

Published pricing guidance is broad because quotes move with page count, design depth, platform, content readiness, eCommerce features and custom functionality. A small brochure website commonly runs from about $2,500 to $7,500, while a polished lead-focused build sits closer to $7,500 to $15,000; stores and heavily custom work start above that.

The useful question is not whether the site will look good, but what is inside the number. Ask for inclusions in plain language:

  • Number of page templates and revision rounds
  • Editable platform, forms and basic search setup
  • Analytics, training, maintenance and backups
  • Discovery, mobile layouts, launch checks and post-launch support

Those inclusions reveal whether a cheaper quote is genuinely lean or just omits work you will pay for later.

Plan the calendar backwards from launch

Timeline guidance is consistent: around 4 to 10 weeks for a simple brochure site, roughly 6 to 10 weeks for a WordPress or small business site, and from about 12 weeks to several months for an eCommerce or custom build. The deciding variable is rarely design speed. It is how quickly content and feedback come back.

Delayed copy, missing images and slow review cycles stretch every phase. If the site supports a campaign, relocation, new service launch or paid ads push, work backwards from the launch date. Confirm core pages before design begins, gather brand assets, photos, service details and offers in one place, nominate a single approver for copy, design and launch items, and separate must-have launch content from improvements that can wait.

Search readiness is a baseline, not a promise

A commercial site should be fast, mobile-first, well structured and built on SEO foundations: clean page structure, mobile usability, sensible on-page copy and quick loading. That baseline gives ongoing marketing a fair chance to work, but it is not a ranking guarantee. Ongoing results depend on competition and content, and no one can honestly promise a specific position under Australian Consumer Law.

So separate build quality from outcome claims. Ask what will be completed at launch, what content is needed afterwards, and how performance will be reviewed. Who this guide applies to

This applies to Brisbane and South East Queensland businesses needing a commercial website built around enquiries, store sales, local visibility or easier updating. It fits SMEs, sole traders, eCommerce operators, businesses planning a redesign, and teams that want WordPress access without a developer on call.

It is less relevant to a temporary one-page placeholder, a fully internal system, or a national enterprise procurement process with separate brand, engineering and governance teams, which need different technical review and contracting steps.

  1. Define the job. Write one sentence naming what the site must achieve: enquiries, store sales, local visibility or easier updating.
  2. Fix scope before pricing. Settle page templates, platform, content readiness and store needs so quotes can be compared like for like.
  3. Request inclusions in plain language. Ask each provider to list discovery, copy support, mobile layouts, technical setup, launch checks and post-launch support.
  4. Work backwards from launch. Set the launch date, assign one approver, and separate must-have content from post-launch improvements.

Common questions

How much should a Brisbane business budget for a new website? For a small brochure website, a common range is about $2,500 to $7,500. A more polished lead-focused build typically sits around $7,500 to $15,000, while eCommerce and heavily custom work starts higher. The final figure depends on page count, design depth, platform, content readiness, store features and custom functionality.

How long does a website build usually take? A simple brochure site is commonly around 4 to 10 weeks, a WordPress or small business site around 6 to 10 weeks, and an eCommerce or custom build from about 12 weeks to several months. Timelines depend most on how quickly content and feedback come back.

Can a web designer guarantee a Google ranking? No. A site can and should be built fast, mobile-first and on sound SEO foundations, but ongoing results depend on competition and content, and no one can honestly guarantee a specific position under Australian Consumer Law.
